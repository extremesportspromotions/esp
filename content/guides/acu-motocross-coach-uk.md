- **What it is:** The ACU Coach Certification Programme, mapped to National Coaching Standards, for anyone who wants to coach or instruct sporting motorcycle skills.
- **What you need first:** Be a competent rider, fully conversant with the skills for your discipline. You may have to pass a riding test first. Commercial coaches must prove a set standard of riding ability. Every coach needs a DBS check.
- **How long it takes:** Commercial coaches attend a 1-day practical assessment and training day. The ACU doesn't publish lengths for the others.
- **Cost:** ACU annual certificate fees: Club Coach £25, Project Coach £35, Commercial Coach £135 including VAT (checked October 2026). Club Coach training is at no cost for members of an affiliated ACU club, with club or centre approval. Project training is subsidised; Commercial training is charged at a commercial rate.
- **What's next:** Coaching track: Club or Project, then Commercial, then ACU Levels 2 and 3. Riding track: [licences](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/acu-motocross-licence-uk). Officials track: [marshal](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/motocross-officials-marshal-uk).

## The three categories

- **Club Coach:** coaches at club level, as a volunteer.
- **Project Coach:** coaches for local authority or community and charity motorcycle projects.
- **Commercial Coach:** coaches for a fee, with a background in motorcycle sport. Commercial coaches can also coach at Club and Project level.

## What every coach signs up to

The ACU says all its coaches:

- have had a Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) check;
- sign up to the ACU Child Protection Code of Practice, its Ethical Code and its Training Manual best practice guidelines.

Commercial coaches also carry first aid qualifications and £5 million public liability insurance.

## Keeping it current

The ACU Training Secretary keeps a register of all certified coaches and runs renewals. You must go to a coach seminar at least once every three years to keep your certification.

## Levels

The ACU says there are three levels of ACU coach. To move to Level 2 or 3, contact the Training Secretary. It doesn't publish the requirements for each level.

> [!SAFETY]
> The NHS says call 999 if someone has hit their head and has been knocked out and not woken up, can't stay awake, has a fit, has new numbness or weakness, has problems with walking, balance or speaking, or hit their head at high speed. That's from the NHS [head injury and concussion](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/head-injury-and-concussion/) page. At an event, tell the nearest marshal straight away.
