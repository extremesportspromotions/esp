- **What it is:** The long skydiving route that comes before any thought of BASE.
- **What you need first:** Nothing to start the skydiving route: a [first course](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-aff-course-uk) at a British Skydiving club is the beginning.
- **How long it takes:** Years, not weeks. Each skydiving licence has its own requirements.
- **Cost:** There's no UK BASE course. First-jump courses abroad are usually £1,250 to £2,500 for 4 to 5 days, and BASE coaching is usually £200 to £400 a day.

## What British Skydiving says

British Skydiving is the governing body for skydiving in the UK. Its Operations Manual (Section 1, para 5) says activities such as fixed-object jumping do not come under the Operations Manual, and British Skydiving does not associate them with the sport of skydiving.

So there is no British Skydiving BASE qualification. We found no UK governing body, coaching award or beginner course for BASE.

## Why skydiving still comes first

British Skydiving sets no BASE entry requirement, and we haven't found an official source that does. What skydiving gives you is a governed, instructed route into parachuting.

The UK route to that is:

- **Start:** an [AFF course](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-aff-course-uk) or the [Category System](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-category-system-uk).
- **Licences:** [A](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-a-licence-uk), [B](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-b-licence-uk), [C](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-c-licence-uk), [D](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-d-licence-uk).
- **Skills:** [disciplines and grades](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-disciplines-and-grades-uk), including canopy training.
- **Kit and costs:** [skydiving kit, costs and safety](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-kit-costs-safety-uk).

## Before you go abroad

There's no UK body checking BASE courses overseas. Ask the provider in writing what experience they need, what their rules are, and who insures you. Never try a jump on your own.

> [!SAFETY]
> If someone is hurt, call 999. The NHS says 999 is for life-threatening emergencies, and the call handler will want the location (postcode or landmarks), what has happened and your number. That's from the NHS [When to call 999](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/urgent-and-emergency-care-services/when-to-call-999/) page.
