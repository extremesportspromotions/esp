## Prices

- **Private lesson or coaching:** usually £32 to £60 an hour on our price list (checked October 2026).
- **British Cycling membership:** Core £52 a year, Premium £82.
- **Full race licence:** £40 senior (18+), £20 junior (16 to 18), no charge under 16, half price from 1 July.
- **Coaching courses:** Introduction to Coaching £305 (Race) or £280 (Freestyle); Coaching in Context £490 (Race) or £530 (Freestyle).

British Cycling's prices were checked in October 2026. It also says entry-level racing bikes are more affordable than many other bike types, and many clubs hire out bikes and kit for tasters.

## Racing kit

British Cycling's list:

- a full-face helmet, compulsory in BMX racing;
- full-finger gloves, which aren't optional;
- long sleeves and covered legs, to save your skin in a tumble;
- knee and elbow pads, recommended.

## Freestyle kit

British Cycling's freestyle coaching course asks for a freestyle BMX in a safe state of repair and a skate-style or full-face helmet, with at least a rear brake for beginner and novice riders.

## Who's in charge

- **England and Great Britain:** British Cycling. Sport England lists BMX under cycling, and British Cycling runs the BMX National Series and Championships.
- **Scotland:** Scottish Cycling.
- **Wales:** Welsh Cycling.
- **Northern Ireland:** Cycling Ulster (Cycling Ireland).

That's from the sports councils' recognition list (September 2025), which puts BMX under cycling.

## Watching and volunteering

Local and regional racing is usually open to watch at no charge, though bigger events sometimes charge. You can help out as a timekeeper, judge or scrutineer, and British Cycling runs volunteer courses.

> [!SAFETY]
> In an emergency, call 999. British Cycling says full-face helmets are compulsory in BMX racing and full-finger gloves are a must. The NHS says call 999 if someone has hit their head and has been knocked out and not woken up, can't stay awake, has a fit, has fallen from more than 1 metre, has problems with walking, balance or speaking, or hit their head at high speed. That's from the NHS [head injury and concussion](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/head-injury-and-concussion/) page.
