- **What it is:** The award for instructing climbing and bouldering sessions on artificial walls.
- **What you need first:** You must be 17 to register (18 for assessment), have at least 6 months of climbing on a variety of walls and be a member of the BMC, Mountaineering Scotland or Mountaineering Ireland.
- **What you'll learn:** The technical, management and teaching skills to run safe, engaging sessions.
- **How long it takes:** 2 days of training and a 1-day assessment (at least six hours). Mountain Training says the average time from training to passing is less than a year.
- **Cost:** Registration is £69. Training and assessment prices are set by providers.
- **What's next:** [Rock Climbing Instructor](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/rock-climbing-instructor-uk) (climbing track)

If you live at the wall, this is how you start sharing it. The award suits wall staff, people running sessions, and young climbers who've completed NICAS Level 4 or 5.

## Before training

- Visits to three different walls on at least 15 occasions, including at least one major public wall.
- 10 led routes at French grade 4 or equivalent.

## Before assessment

- 30 personal climbing visits to at least three different walls.
- 40 led climbs on walls, and confidence leading at French grade 4.
- Helping to supervise at least 15 instructed sessions on at least two different walls, including a large public facility, with your own reflective comments on at least five of them in DLOG.
- A current first aid certificate from an in-person course of at least one full day (8 hours) with an assessment.

Mountain Training also offers a Bouldering Wall Instructor award and an abseil module for Climbing Wall Instructors.

> [!SAFETY]
> Indoors still means ropes, heights and falls. Most accidents start with a missed check.
