A tandem skydive is the simplest way to experience freefall for the first time. You don't need any previous experience or a long training course: you're securely attached to a qualified instructor who takes care of the technical side. Here's what to expect, from booking to landing. We also cover indoor skydiving, where you float on a column of air inside a wind tunnel.

## How a tandem skydive works

On a tandem you're harnessed to a professional instructor, who controls the exit, freefall, parachute deployment and landing. You climb to altitude in an aircraft and exit together. At Skydive Langar, for example, you exit at around 14,000 feet and reach speeds of over 120mph in freefall before your instructor opens the parachute. Exact heights and timings vary between centres, so ask yours when you book.

You are attached to an instructor who holds a British Skydiving tandem rating. One centre, Skydive Langar, explains that its parachute systems carry two parachutes, a main and a reserve, with the main repacked after every jump and the reserve inspected and repacked every 6 months, plus further backup systems in the kit.

## Before you book: age, health and weight

In the UK, tandem jumps take place at drop zones affiliated to **British Skydiving** and are run to its standards and procedures. Its key requirements are:

- **Age.** You must be at least 16. If you're under 18, you need your parent's or guardian's written permission, and your medical declaration must be witnessed by your parent or legal guardian.
- **Upper age.** British Skydiving's upper age limit for starting solo training (before your 55th birthday) doesn't apply to tandem students, although health requirements still do.
- **Health.** Every tandem participant completes a medical self-declaration, British Skydiving's Form 115A. If you can't agree with every statement on it, you should postpone your jump until you've had qualified medical advice and a doctor has signed Form 115B. The form is valid for a year, as long as nothing changes. The centre and your instructor can't give medical advice.
- **Weight.** Each centre sets its own weight limits based on its risk assessments. British Skydiving says maximums are typically around 15 stone, and it doesn't keep a list, so contact the centre directly.

If you're unsure about anything health-related, speak to your GP well before the day.

## Choosing a centre

Choose a centre affiliated to British Skydiving, known as an Affiliated Parachute Training Organisation (PTO). British Skydiving says there are 27 in the UK, all following the same safety standards, and its website has a "Find your nearest PTO" tool. Beyond that, ask:

- What's included in the price, and are photos or video extra?
- How long should I expect to be on site?
- What happens if the weather stops jumping on my booked day?
- Are there any restrictions on my height, weight or fitness?


## On the day

You'll check in, hand over your medical form and complete the centre's paperwork. Form 115A must be handed to the centre in person, not sent to British Skydiving, and you'll re-confirm it just before your jump.

Next comes your briefing. British Skydiving says it covers safety procedures, equipment and what to expect. Listen carefully, especially to what your instructor says about your body position and landing.

Then you may have to wait. Skydiving is very weather-dependent: Skydive Langar explains that centres must operate within strict British Skydiving limits on cloud and wind, and if conditions aren't suitable you'll be offered another date.

> [!SAFETY]
> Be completely honest on your medical form. You must tell the instructors if you've ever partly or fully dislocated a shoulder; it doesn't automatically stop you jumping, but it lets your instructor help reduce the risk of it happening again. Colds or sore throats can make you temporarily unfit to jump, and British Skydiving's rules say anyone under the influence of alcohol won't be allowed to jump. Follow every instruction in your briefing, and ask about anything that's unclear before you board. In an emergency, call 999: the NHS says 999 is for life-threatening emergencies.

## The jump itself

After the climb, you'll leave the aircraft with your instructor in the position you were briefed on. Freefall is over quickly. Skydive Langar says many people expect a rollercoaster "sinking" feeling, but that most describe it as feeling more like flying.

Your instructor then deploys the parachute for a canopy ride of several minutes, with time to take in the view. For landing, do exactly what you practised in your briefing.

## What to wear and bring

- Comfortable clothes you can move in, suited to the weather, with warm layers and gloves on a cold day.
- Your completed medical form.

## If you catch the bug

Many people jump once for the experience; others want more. British Skydiving offers two routes to learning to skydive solo: Accelerated Freefall (AFF) and the Category System (often called static line). Many people qualify for a British Skydiving A Licence in around 18 jumps. Under British Skydiving's rules, you can even switch from tandem to AFF at Level 2 after at least 2 tandem jumps, with conversion training first.

## Indoor skydiving in a wind tunnel

Indoor skydiving is flying on a strong column of air inside a vertical wind tunnel. It recreates the feeling of freefall, with no aircraft and no parachute. British Skydiving's members' insurance doesn't cover indoor skydiving at wind tunnels, so it's a separate world with its own rules.

iFLY runs several UK tunnels, including Manchester, Milton Keynes, Basingstoke, Birmingham and London at The O2. Its first-time flyer packages include instruction from instructors qualified by the International Bodyflight Association (IBA), which iFLY describes as the independent governing body of the sport. Each flight lasts around 55 seconds. iFLY asks you to arrive 45 minutes before your flight and says to expect to be on site for about an hour and a half.

A tunnel taster of two flights typically costs £64 to £90 in the UK. The price depends on the package, the location and when you fly.

iFLY says flyers can be aged 3 or over, as long as the flight suit and helmet fit. Anyone under 18 must have a parent or guardian with them on the day, and that adult signs the waiver for them.

Every flyer completes an online waiver before arriving, and it includes your pre-flight training. iFLY will not fly you if you have ever dislocated a shoulder, are pregnant, are wearing a hard cast, are under the influence of alcohol or non-prescribed drugs, or have a current or past head, neck or back injury or condition, or a heart condition, that flying could make worse. The upper weight limit is 21 stone 4 lb (136kg). Wear lace-up trainers and take off all jewellery and loose items.
