Finding a coach is the exciting bit, but it pays to know who you're learning from. Here's what UK governing bodies say about choosing a qualified, insured instructor, the questions to ask before you book, and exactly how ESP's coach matching and free club map work.

## Why a qualified coach matters

Extreme sports carry real risk. Our own enquiry form puts it plainly: "Extreme sports carry inherent risk of injury. Training with a professional coach does not remove that risk, but it helps you learn safer technique, use proper gear, and progress at a pace that fits your level." [1]

Here's what UK governing bodies and professional associations say about starting out:

- **Paragliding and hang gliding.** The British Hang Gliding and Paragliding Association (BHPA) says initial training must be undertaken at a BHPA registered school [2], and calls hang gliders and paragliders "fully fledged aircraft" that need carefully structured professional training [3].
- **Skydiving.** British Skydiving says training is delivered by qualified instructors at its 27 affiliated Parachute Training Organisations (PTOs), which all follow the same safety standards [4]. Even on a tandem, it's honest that "there is always a small but definite risk of injury" [5].
- **Scuba diving.** A British Sub-Aqua Club (BSAC) Try Dive is a pool dive with a BSAC-qualified instructor, and you'll complete a medical form first [6].
- **Paddling.** Paddle UK is the new name for British Canoeing, adopted in March 2024 [7]. Its Go Paddling finder only lists clubs affiliated to a national governing body [8].
- **Climbing and mountaineering.** Guidance published by the Association of Mountaineering Instructors (AMI) says there's no comprehensive legal requirement for someone offering professional services in the mountains to hold a particular qualification [9]. The British Mountaineering Council (BMC) notes that the professional associations list qualified members [10].
- **Snowsports.** Snowsport England's licence requires coaches and instructors to hold a valid qualification, a first aid certificate, safeguarding training, a DBS check and ongoing professional development (CPD) [11].
- **Surfing.** Surfing England, the national governing body for surfing in England, has a "Learn to surf" section to help you find a surf school [12].

### Don't forget insurance

The AMI guidance suggests checking that a professional has current professional liability or indemnity insurance, and looking elsewhere if there's no evidence of it [9]. Mountain Training says instructors working in a paid capacity should have insurance [13]. The BHPA says students at its registered schools carry £5 million third-party insurance through BHPA membership (as of September 2026) [3].

## Questions to ask a coach before you book

A good coach will happily answer these:

1. **What qualification do you hold, and who issued it?** You can check with the issuing body or a professional association's member list [10]. Ask about their CPD and experience too, as a qualification alone doesn't prove someone is up to date [9].
2. **Is your licence current, and does it cover what I want to do?** Snowsport England, for example, gives each licence a "remit" that sets where a coach can work and who they can coach [11].
3. **Are you insured for coaching?** Ask what kind of insurance it is and whether it's current.
4. **Do you have a current first aid certificate?** Snowsport England requires one for a licence, and British Cycling requires one for its coaching insurance cover [14].
5. **Have you done safeguarding training, and do you have a DBS check?** This matters most if you're booking for a child or vulnerable adult. Snowsport England requires both for a licence, and only issues enhanced DBS checks [15].
6. **Are you registered with or affiliated to the governing body?** Look for a BHPA registered school, a British Skydiving affiliated PTO or an affiliated paddling club.
7. **Booking for someone under 18?** Providers paid to run in-scope climbing, caving, trekking or watersports for under-18s must by law hold an adventure activities licence, with some exemptions. You can check the Health and Safety Executive's public register [16].

> [!SAFETY]
> Always learn with a qualified, insured instructor, and never try a high-risk sport on your own without proper training. Answer every medical form honestly, tell your coach about injuries or conditions, and remember that your instructor can't give you medical clearance: that's a job for a medical professional [5][17]. If a provider can't show you their qualification or insurance, look elsewhere.

## How ESP coach matching works

### Step by step

1. **Tell us your sport.** Fill in the [enquiry form](https://esp-lemon.vercel.app/#enquire), which takes about a minute. It asks about your sport, level, goals, age and location, including how far you'll travel [1]. Sending it is free: no charge is taken when you submit it [18].
2. **We get in touch.** We open a line of communication with you and start looking for the right coach or club [18].
3. **Pay the matching fee.** The payment link is the last stage of your enquiry.
4. **Train with a pro.**

Prefer email? You can write to extremesportspromotionsuk@gmail.com [1].

### Who we work with

All our coaches are UK-based. Before we introduce you, we check the qualifications, insurance and references of the coaches and clubs we work with [18]. That doesn't replace your own checks, so still ask the questions above.

### How long it takes

We aim to secure you a place within 5 working days of your enquiry. That's an aim, not a guarantee: timing depends on things like how far you're willing to travel (including whether you'd travel nationally), busy seasons and bad weather [18].

### The fee and refunds

Our matching fee is £29.99 to find and introduce you to the right coach; coach session fees are separate. (Price correct as of 26 September 2026.) [18]

We refund the matching fee if we can't provide a coach, or if your health means you're unable to train.

### Under-18s

If the student is under 18, we need consent from a parent or guardian, and Matthew Thornton, ESP's owner, contacts them personally. A parent or guardian must travel with the child to the club. For some of these sports, we highly recommend that parents and guardians take part too [18].

## Using the club map

The [club map](https://esp-lemon.vercel.app/#find-a-club) lists UK clubs, centres and schools that offer coaching, lessons or courses. As of 25 September 2026 it included 223 venues, each with a working website and contact details [1].

- **Filter by sport**, then open a pin for the venue's website, phone number or email.
- **Jump straight to a sport** with a link like `https://esp-lemon.vercel.app/?sport=skydiving#find-a-club`.
- **Check before you travel.** Listings come from public sources, including OpenStreetMap, BHPA club listings and British Skydiving drop zone directories. They were last checked in September 2026, and map positions are approximate.

The map is a starting point, not a complete directory: there are no venues yet for kiteboarding or BASE jumping, and only a few for motocross, wingsuit flying and kayaking. A listing isn't a review or an endorsement. The paragliding pins are currently BHPA recreational clubs, so do your first training at a BHPA registered school [2], though the BHPA encourages trainees to contact their local club early [3].

> [!TIP]
> Not after a coach? Browsing the club map is free, with no matching fee.
