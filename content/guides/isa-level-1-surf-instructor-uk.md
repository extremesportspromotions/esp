- **What it is:** The International Surfing Association's (ISA) award for teaching beginner and early intermediate surfers.
- **What you need first:** The ISA Introduction to Surf Instructing e-learning course, which has no prerequisites.
- **What you'll learn:** Safety, kit for beginners, coaching methods, skill development and planning.
- **How long it takes:** About 8 to 10 hours online, then the Level 1 course, then 20 logged practical hours.
- **Cost:** The ISA lists the e-learning course at $100 (checked October 2026). The full Level 1 course usually runs £160 to £400.
- **What's next:** In England, [Surf Instructor: Recreation](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/surf-instructor-recreation-award-uk) through Recognised Prior Learning, then [Surf Coach: Progression](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/surf-coach-progression-award-uk).

The ISA is the world governing body for surfing, recognised by the IOC. Scottish Surfing runs its courses, and Surfing England recognises its Level 1.

## What you need to qualify

- Finish the ISA e-learning course.
- Take part in the Surf Level 1 course with an ISA Course Presenter.
- Show a basic level of surfing: go left and right on green waves and exit a wave with control.
- Complete 20 practical hours, logged and supervised by an ISA Instructor.
- Get a valid lifesaving or surf rescue award.
- Sign the ISA Coach's Code of Conduct.

## Where you'll find it in the UK

- **Scotland:** Scottish Surfing runs ISA Level 1 courses. In 2026 they're at no cost to members of Scottish Surfing affiliated surf clubs.
- **Wales:** the Welsh Surfing Federation is the governing body for surfing in Wales.
- **England:** Surfing England recognises ISA Level 1 through Recognised Prior Learning. You get its Surf Instructor: Recreation award (SE1). It also says that from 2028 it can no longer accept international and foreign surf coach certificates.

> [!SAFETY]
> Caught in a rip? Don't swim against it. Stay with your board, or Float to Live if you don't have one. If you can stand, wade, don't swim. Wade or swim parallel to the shore until you're free, then head for shore. Raise your hand and shout for help. See someone else in trouble? Alert the lifeguards or call 999 or 112 and ask for the coastguard. That's the [RNLI's advice](https://rnli.org/safety/know-the-risks/rip-currents).
