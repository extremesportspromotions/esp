Kiteboarding, also called kitesurfing, is riding a board along the water while a wind-powered kite pulls you through a harness, with no motor and no fixed link between the kite and the board. A beginner starts with a lesson at an approved school, learning to fly the kite before using the board, not with jumps.

## A first session

The British Kitesports Association is the national governing body Sport England recognises for kite surfing. British Kitesports says it sets the standards for learning to kite at its schools, and it has around 30 recognised schools in the UK.

You start with a qualified instructor at an approved school. The introduction to flying the kite takes about one to two days: basic kite handling, then rigging, launching and basic flying, then body dragging to generate power, with control and a self-rescue. The board is introduced only after you can fly the kite, for the start and the first rides. Most people can be up and riding after a three-day course at an approved school.

## What to take

In UK waters you need a wetsuit for the time of year. You need a harness to spread the pull of the kite. A seat harness, with the hook lower down, is the style described as giving good support while you learn. In a lesson, a helmet and an impact jacket or buoyancy aid are mandatory, and they are recommended while you are learning.

> [!SAFETY]
> A bar and kite have a safety system that releases the kite's power without letting the kite go, by easing all but one line or by letting the kite flag. If you are unsure about a session, do not go: ask a more experienced rider or, preferably, a local school or instructor.

Governing body: [British Kitesports Association (BKSA)](https://britishkitesports.org). It sets the UK safety standards and training awards for kiteboarding, so it's the official place to find out more. Many schools also use IKO courses.
