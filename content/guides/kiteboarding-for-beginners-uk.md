Kiteboarding, also called kitesurfing, is riding a board along the water while a wind-powered kite pulls you through a harness, with no motor and no fixed link between the kite and the board [1]. A beginner starts with a lesson at an approved school, learning to fly the kite before using the board, not with jumps [2][1].

## A first session

The British Kitesports Association is the national governing body Sport England recognises for kite surfing [5]. British Kitesports says it sets the standards for learning to kite at its schools, and it has around 30 recognised schools in the UK [3][2].

You start with a qualified instructor at an approved school [2]. The introduction to flying the kite takes about one to two days: basic kite handling, then rigging, launching and basic flying, then body dragging to generate power, with control and a self-rescue [2]. The board is introduced only after you can fly the kite, for the start and the first rides [1][2]. Most people can be up and riding after a three-day course at an approved school [2].

## What to take

In UK waters you need a wetsuit for the time of year [1]. You need a harness to spread the pull of the kite [1]. A seat harness, with the hook lower down, is the style described as giving good support while you learn [1]. In a lesson, a helmet and an impact jacket or buoyancy aid are mandatory, and they are recommended while you are learning [1].

> [!SAFETY]
> A bar and kite have a safety system that releases the kite's power without letting the kite go, by easing all but one line or by letting the kite flag [1]. If you are unsure about a session, do not go: ask a more experienced rider or, preferably, a local school or instructor [4].
