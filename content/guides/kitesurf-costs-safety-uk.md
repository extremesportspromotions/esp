BKSA says most people can be up and riding after a 3-day course at an approved school. Here's what it costs and how to stay safe while you get there.

## Prices

- A taster or intro day with kit: £130 to £199.
- Beginner to independent rider: £360 to £1,000. A short group course is the low end, a private zero-to-hero the high end.
- A private lesson: £65 to £80 an hour. Schools often sell this as £130 to £149 for two hours.
- BKSA membership: £52 a year, £44 for students and £38 for juniors. It includes third-party liability and personal accident cover, plus up to £1,000 of kit cover.

## Who runs what

- **British Kitesports Association (BKSA):** the national governing body for kitesports in the UK, formally recognised by the home countries' sports councils. It runs instructor training, recognises schools (around 30) and affiliates clubs.
- **RYA:** runs instructor awards for wingsurfing and wingfoiling.
- **GKA:** runs the Kite World Tour, sanctioned by World Sailing.
- **RNLI and HM Coastguard:** safety and rescue. The RNLI works with BKSA on kitesurf safety.

## The RNLI checklist

- Always kite with another person. If you go alone, take a way to call or signal for help.
- Never ride out further than you can swim back. Equipment failure does happen.
- Check the conditions and tides. Don't go out in conditions you can't handle.
- Check what kite sizes other riders are using. No correct size? Don't go out.
- Don't let someone who doesn't know kites help you launch or land. Show them how first.
- Tell someone when you're going out and when you'll be back.
- Wear a buoyancy aid and helmet, and carry a way to call for help, like a phone in a waterproof pouch.
- Follow BKSA's safety advice.

> [!SAFETY]
> In trouble at the coast? Call 999 or 112 and ask for the coastguard. Inland, on a river or lake, ask for Fire and Rescue. That's the [RNLI's advice](https://rnli.org/water-safety/how-to-call-for-help-at-sea).
