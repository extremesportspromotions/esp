Mountain biking is cycling off road, on forest trails and purpose-built tracks. A beginner starts at a trail centre, on an easy waymarked loop or with a coach.

## A first session

A trail centre is the simplest place to start. British Cycling describes them as one-stop shops, usually on forestry land, with waymarked, graded routes and bike hire.

Forestry England's Forest of Dean Cycle Centre in Gloucestershire is one example, not a review. Its green trails are described as suitable for families and first-timers. The centre lists approved coaches for someone on a first bike. You book the coach, and prices vary.

A led ride is not a skills lesson. A first session should be an easy graded loop, or a coached session with a British Cycling coach.

Cross-country, downhill and four-cross are all part of the sport. None of them is a first session.

## Hire before you buy

You do not need to own a bike. At the Forest of Dean you can hire one, and a helmet, and you should book ahead at weekends and in school holidays. Hire an easy-trail bike before you buy one.

> [!TIP]
> Use a hire bike and a hire helmet for the first visit. Buy your own kit once you know you will come back.

## Helmet and gloves

On Forestry England trails, always wear a helmet and gloves. The Highway Code says you should wear a cycle helmet that fits and is securely fastened. It says "should", not "must".

## Fitness

British Cycling says skill matters as much as fitness, and riders come in all shapes, sizes and ages. Stay on an easy grade. If a condition might affect your balance or breathing, speak to your doctor first.

## Trail colours

Colours are a guide, not a promise, and not every UK venue uses the same words. Forestry England's scale, updated on 5 July 2023, is green (easy: relatively flat, wide and smooth, though it can be loose or muddy), blue (moderate), red (difficult), black (severe) and double-black (extreme, with obstacles you cannot ride around). Check the forest you are visiting, and start on green.
