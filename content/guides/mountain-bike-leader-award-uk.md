- **What it is:** British Cycling's highest leadership award, developed from the old Level 3 award.
- **What you need first:** British Cycling membership and age 18. You need the Trail Leader, Level 2 MTB Leadership or Trail Cycle Leader award, or the Recognition of Prior Learning direct-entry route. You also need riding skills generally comparable to black-graded trails, including drop-offs and bunny hops.
- **What you'll learn:** Advanced leadership, mountain weather, advanced navigation and repairs, and managing incidents in remote, technical terrain.
- **How long it takes:** 2 to 3 hours of e-learning and 3 days of training, then an online module and a 2-day assessment. You have up to two years after training to be assessed.
- **Cost:** British Cycling lists training at £550 to £750 and assessment at £400 to £550, set by each provider.
- **What's next:** Leader track: [Kit, costs and safety](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/mtb-kit-costs-safety-uk).

Wild hills, big days and a group that trusts you completely. British Cycling calls this award the gold standard for anyone who wants to be among the best MTB guides in Britain.

## Your logbook

On or before your training day, show evidence of riding in wild, mountain terrain in the last 18 months, plus experience leading rides. E-bikes aren't permitted on this award because of the bike handling, hike-a-bike and water crossings involved.

## Once you've passed

You can lead group rides on technical trails in remote places, in intermediate weather. To be covered by British Cycling insurance once you've passed, you need to be a current paid British Cycling member, hold a two-day outdoor first aid certificate, and complete safeguarding training appropriate to the country you lead in.

Want wilder rides without harder trails? British Cycling's Remote Award takes Off-Road Leaders, Trail Leaders and Trail Cycle Leaders (18+) more than a 30-minute walk from a road, while keeping their award's technical remit. It's a 3-day training and assessment course.

> [!SAFETY]
> In remote terrain, help can be hours away. Carry what you'd need to look after someone until it arrives.
