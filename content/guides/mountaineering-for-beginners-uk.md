There are many ways to explore Britain's mountains, hills and coastlines. Mountaineering, scrambling, hiking, rock climbing, camping and coasteering are all different skills. Learn each one properly, piece them together, and you'll become a formidable wild traveller.

## Your first steps

The British Mountaineering Council (BMC) represents climbers, hill walkers and mountaineers in England and Wales.

If you're a complete beginner, practise navigating on low ground first, and then take on bigger hikes as your confidence grows. Going solo carries real risks. A fall, a slip or a simple twisted ankle can quickly become serious, especially if no one knows where you are. Our advice is simple: take it easy, ideally with a friend. If you can't, join a club or book a coach. It's the safest way to progress, and you'll meet people who love the same things you do.

Mountain Training's two-day [Hill Skills and Mountain Skills](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/hill-and-mountain-skills-uk) courses cover planning a walk, what to take, navigation, common hazards and, most importantly, what to do in an emergency. You can start from age 10, and Hill Skills needs no experience at all.

## Coasteering

Coasteering is scrambling along the rocky edge of the sea. You climb over sharp rock, then leap from ledges into a wild ocean while the waves crash around you. The National Coasteering Charter (NCC) defines it as swimming, jumping, scrambling and traversing along the foreshore under the guidance of a coasteering guide. Only ever do it with a guide.

The NCC says you should wear a wetsuit, a personal flotation device, a helmet and closed-toe shoes secured to your feet.

The NCC represents and advises coasteering providers across the UK, and runs a national award for coasteering guides. It says most people have their first go as part of a programme with an adventure activity provider, and that this is the best way to learn the basics safely. You can't coasteer just anywhere, because the weather, the sea and the coast itself all limit where it's safe. Your guide picks the stretch of coast on the day.

A coached coasteering session usually costs £55 to £150 per person.

Fancy guiding one day? The NCC's Coasteering Guide Award starts with a training course, which asks for at least 10 logged water hours of coasteering. To be assessed you must be 18 or over, be an individual NCC member and have at least 30 hours of coasteering experience, at least 24 of them assisting experienced guides or leading under supervision or with peer groups, at venues in at least two NCC regions.

The RNLI says never to jump into the sea on your own. Water depth changes with the tide, so it can be shallower than it looks, rocks and other objects can hide under the surface, and cold water can shock your body. In an emergency, call 999 or 112 and ask for the Coastguard.

## Rock climbing

Most people take their first climbing steps on an indoor wall, not real rock. Walls offer two types of climbing:

- **Bouldering.** Short climbs, called problems, a few metres off the ground, with a thick mat to land on.
- **Top-roping.** Taller climbs on a rope that runs through an anchor at the top. Your partner on the ground, the belayer, holds the rope through a belay device and catches you if you slip.

An indoor wall is the best place to learn. Start with an intro session with a coach, so you learn to climb safely and keep your partner safe too.

Once you're comfortable indoors, you can head outside, from gentle first climbs to some of the sheerest, most intense routes you can imagine. Mountain Training's one-day Rock Skills Introduction needs no experience and starts at age 10. See [Rock Skills Introduction and Intermediate](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/rock-skills-courses-uk). An outdoor intro climb usually costs £55 to £150 per person.

> [!SAFETY]
> Mountain weather can change very quickly. The Met Office says it can vary dramatically from one hour to the next and from one hill to the next, so use a forecast made for hill and mountain users, such as the Met Office mountain forecast, and turn around if you are tired, the weather is worse than you expected, or you are running out of time. In an accident, Mountain Rescue England and Wales says to dial 999 or 112, ask for the police and then mountain rescue, give the details you have prepared, and do not change your position until the rescue team contacts you.
