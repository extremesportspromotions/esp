## Prices

Typical UK school prices:

- An experience day: £140 to £230 per person. BHPA day membership is sometimes extra.
- Getting qualified (EP plus CP): £1,250 to £1,700. Annual BHPA membership is extra.
- A day's training: £140 to £200.

BHPA fees (from 1 April 2026):

- One Day Introductory Membership: £33, or £5 if you're under 21.
- Trial Membership: £120 for 3 months, with no joining fee.
- Annual Flying Membership: £192, plus a £35 joining fee. It's £172 for concessions (21 and under, 67 and over, or disabled).
- Each rating (CP, Pilot, Advanced Pilot) or added environment: £20.
- IPPI card for flying abroad: £20.
- Club Coach course: £35, or £25 for a retake. The Instructor course is £450 and the Senior Instructor course is £200.

## Who runs what

- **BHPA:** oversees pilot and instructor training standards, supports the UK's clubs and registered schools, and provides airworthiness standards and coaching courses for hang gliding and paragliding.
- **BHPA registered schools:** where all initial training must happen. They're monitored for safety, training quality and equipment, and their instructors are licensed by the BHPA.
- **Clubs:** look after flying sites, and many offer club coaching.
- **FAI and CIVL:** the international level. The IPPI card comes from the FAI, and you get it through the BHPA office.

## Safety basics

- You must be at least 14 to fly solo or hold a BHPA rating. Under-18s need a parent's or guardian's consent form.
- You don't need a medical, but you should be fit and in good health. If you have a condition such as epilepsy, fainting, high blood pressure, a heart condition or diabetes, ask your doctor first.
- Membership includes £5 million third-party insurance, but not personal accident cover. Get personal or travel insurance from an independent broker.
- You need the Pilot rating to fly cross-country, partly so you can stay clear of controlled airspace.
- Poor signal? Register for emergency SMS before you go by texting "register" to 999.

> [!SAFETY]
> Emergency on a hill? Dial 999 or 112, ask for the Police, then Mountain Rescue. On the coast, ask for the Coastguard. That's from the [BHPA's emergency sheet](https://www.bhpa.co.uk/documents/index.php?doc=In_an_Emergency.pdf).
