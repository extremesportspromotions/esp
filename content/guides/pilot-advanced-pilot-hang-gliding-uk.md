- **What it is:** Pilot is the BHPA's 'fully qualified' rating. Advanced Pilot is for the above-average pilot who has mastered their glider.
- **What you need first:** Club Pilot (Novice) for Pilot. The Pilot rating for Advanced Pilot.
- **What you'll learn:** Thermalling, landing accuracy, air law, navigation and weather, then cross-country flying.
- **Cost:** £20 for each rating (BHPA fees from 1 April 2026). The online mock exams are at no cost.
- **What's next:** Pilot track: [competitions](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/hang-gliding-competitions-uk), then FAI Delta badges. Passenger track: [Dual Pilot](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/hang-gliding-dual-pilot-uk). Coaching and teaching track: [coaches and instructors](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/hang-gliding-coaches-instructors-uk) (Instructor needs Pilot plus 25 launches in the environment).

## Pilot (P)

This is the BHPA's 'fully qualified' rating, and it's your ticket to cross-country. After CP you carry on learning at a club, not at a school, with coaches or instructors guiding your flying tasks.

**Hill pilots:**
- 3 take-offs and 3 landings in winds under 5mph.
- 2 top landings at each of three different sites.
- Flights from 5 different sites, at least 3 of them inland.
- At least 3 flights of over an hour.
- At least 50 flights and 25 hours logged.

**Tow pilots:**
- At least 75 flights, with at least 30 to above 800ft.
- 10 flights in each of 4 wind directions, and 3 flights from each of 2 sites.
- 5 flights of over 10 minutes after releasing no higher than 1,500ft.

**Everyone:**
- 5 controlled landings within 15m of a target, from flights of at least 3 minutes.
- Show you understand slow flight and its dangers.
- Co-ordinated 360° turns both ways.
- 3 thermal climbs of over 1,000ft.
- Fly safely with others, keep a good look-out and follow the Rules of the Air.

**The exam:** sit it once your flying tasks are done, with a Coach or Instructor who holds the rating as invigilator. Hill pilots can take five short online papers (meteorology, air law, principles of flight in a hang gliding version, human factors and airmanship, and navigation), with 75% needed on each. The other option is one paper of 1 hour 45 minutes, with 70% needed in each section, and that's the only option for tow pilots. Pilots with a motor (self-propelled hang gliders) usually sit the Pilot (Power) paper. The BHPA's no-cost mock exams online show you the level.

Without Pilot, you can't fly cross-country under BHPA rules and its insurance won't cover you. The only exceptions are briefed corridor tasks at BHPA-sanctioned events, or one set up for you by an Instructor or Senior Coach.

## Advanced Pilot (AP)

Every pilot must:

1. Gain broad experience in a wide range of conditions.
2. Log experience on a range of models (at least three for hang gliding).
3. Log 100 hours.
4. Fly at least 100km cross-country using thermal lift.
5. Pass the Advanced Pilot theory exam.
6. Gain experience in another environment (tow, hill, aerotow or power).
7. Attend a BHPA Club Coach course.
8. Show good airmanship and the right attitude.

On top of that, hang glider pilots must:

- Fly a defined cross-country of at least 75km, out and return or triangle, using thermal lift.
- Land accurately every time: 3 landings within 30m.

The BHPA also expects lots of experience in mountain and dynamic soaring. AP matches stage 5 of the FAI Safe Pro scheme (Safe Pro Delta is the hang gliding card). Beyond AP, the next goals are the FAI's Delta badges for distance, height gain and duration.

Already a paraglider pilot? You train on a hang glider from the start, but you don't re-sit Pilot or AP exams you've passed.

> [!SAFETY]
> Emergency on the hill? Dial 999 or 112 and ask for the Police, then Mountain Rescue. On the coast, ask for the Coastguard. That's the advice on the [BHPA's emergency sheet](https://www.bhpa.co.uk/documents/index.php?doc=In_an_Emergency.pdf).
