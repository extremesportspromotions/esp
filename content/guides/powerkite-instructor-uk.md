- **What it is:** The BKSA award for teaching kites on land, with a fast-track route that covers powerkite, landboard and buggy.
- **What you need first:** BKSA membership, an in-person first aid course, kite flying experience and 12 hours of shadow teaching.
- **What you'll learn:** Safety, communication and the full BKSA teaching scheme.
- **How long it takes:** Two days, often with an evening session on day one.
- **What's next:** Senior Powerkite Instructor, or take it to the water with [Level 1 kitesurf instructor](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/kitesurf-instructor-level-1-uk).

BKSA calls powerkiting the fundamental skill of every kitesport. Teach it well and you're teaching the start of everything.

## Powerkite Instructor prerequisites

- Valid British Kitesports membership.
- A valid first aid qualification from at least a 1-day attended course. Online courses aren't accepted.
- Riding or kite flying proficiency and experience.
- 12 hours of shadow teaching at a British Kitesports school, or prior teaching experience.

BKSA can put you in touch with centres for this training, and it must be done before the course starts. Pass the course and any action plan, and you can teach 6 people with three kites at a BKSA recognised school, club or local-authority-recognised site.

## Senior Powerkite Instructor prerequisites

- Valid British Kitesports membership.
- A valid first aid qualification.
- Riding or kite flying proficiency and experience.
- Powerkite Instructor.
- 150 logged hours.
- Teaching for at least 6 months.
- 2 years of kite flying.

The two-day course covers hiring staff, safety systems, running a school and handling customers and bad weather. Every BKSA recognised school needs a Senior Instructor as its training principal.

> [!SAFETY]
> Flying on the beach? If anyone gets into trouble in the water, call 999 or 112 and ask for the coastguard. That's the [RNLI's advice](https://rnli.org/water-safety/how-to-call-for-help-at-sea).
