## Typical prices

- **Try dive** (PADI Discover Scuba or a centre try dive, kit included): usually £55 to £160. The top of that range is a private session.
- **PADI or SSI Open Water at a UK centre:** usually £445 to £900, including pool, open-water dives and certification. A drysuit add-on is often extra.
- **Guided or instructional dive:** usually £65 to £120. Diving is sold by the dive.

## Fitness to dive

- BSAC divers fill in a self-declaration medical form when they join, every year when they renew, and if a medical condition comes up in between.
- The form is a requirement set by the UK Diving Medical Committee (UKDMC), a network of medical referees with accredited diving medicine expertise.
- Answer no to everything and you don't need to do anything else. Answer yes to anything and you'll need to contact a medical referee.
- If you'd like a medical anyway, you can see your own GP or a UKDMC referee. Only a UKDMC referee can endorse a UKDMC fitness-to-dive certificate.
- Diving at work (including paid instructing) needs an HSE medical instead.

## Who's in charge

- **BSAC:** calls itself the National Governing Body for scuba diving and snorkelling in the UK. Sport England and Sport Wales both list it for sub aqua.
- **Scotland:** the sports councils' joint list names the Scottish Sub Aqua Club for Scotland, with BSAC for England and the UK.
- **HSE:** the Diving at Work Regulations 1997 cover dives where someone is at work, such as a paid instructor.

> [!SAFETY]
> Suspect decompression illness or a lung injury? At sea, call the Coastguard on VHF DSC or Channel 16, or dial 999. Inland in England, Wales or Northern Ireland, call the BHA/RN Diver helpline on 07831 151523 (24 hours). Inland in Scotland, call Aberdeen Royal Infirmary on 0345 408 6008 (24 hours). That's from [BSAC's incident guidance](https://www.bsac.com/safety/diving-incidents/in-the-event-of-a-diving-incident/).
