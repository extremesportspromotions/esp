Skateboarding is standing on a short wooden board with small wheels and moving it by pushing one foot on the ground. A beginner starts by standing on the board on grass or carpet, to get used to standing sideways, then finds a smooth, flat space to roll. Do not go to a skatepark until you can roll with confidence, unless you are there for a lesson. This guide also covers BMX, which you can try at a track with a hired bike.

## A first session

Skateboard GB’s learn-to-skate guide sets out those first steps. Skateboard GB describes itself as the national governing body for skateboarding, and Sport England does not list a recognised body for skateboarding in England.

Press your toes down to turn one way and your heels to turn the other, and stop by pressing your pushing foot on the ground. Left foot forward is called regular, and right foot forward is called goofy, and you use whichever foot feels comfortable at the front.

You can also start in a lesson. Find a local skate school, or book private lessons with a Skateboard GB approved instructor. Lessons are mainly at skateparks, or run by skate schools at skateparks or other suitable places, and they cover pushing, turning and learning tricks.

## What to take

Buy a proper board from a specialist skate shop, or from a specialist online shop. Toy boards, including many sold by high-street sports shops and toy shops, are poor quality and can put you off. A complete board, already built and ready to ride, is a straightforward first board. If you have never stood on a board, a bigger board is usually more stable.

Wear skate shoes if you can get them, or trainers with flat soles. Running trainers arch your foot and can throw you off balance. Wear comfortable, loose clothes, and trousers you do not mind ripping, because you may fall while you practise.

Wear a helmet while you are learning, and pads can help, especially wrist guards. Pads and guards are recommended because falling is part of learning. Most skate schools and lessons should include a board, a helmet and pads, so ask before you assume you must bring them. Many indoor skateparks rent boards.

> [!SAFETY]
> A helmet should fit snug on your head. If it is too loose, it will not protect you.

## BMX

BMX is riding a small, strong bike, usually with 20-inch wheels, a single gear and often just a rear brake. It covers both racing and freestyle tricks. BMX racing takes place on purpose-built tracks made of compacted dirt and tarmac. A track is around 400 metres long, with a start ramp, jumps and banked corners called berms. British Cycling is the national governing body for cycling in Great Britain, and its BMX pages list clubs and events.

Most active BMX clubs run taster and skills sessions at their local track. Many hire out a bike, a helmet and protective gear, so you can try it before you spend money. There are small bikes for young children, and older riders take part too.

At Lee Valley VeloPark in east London, a 60-minute BMX Learn to Ride session is listed at £17.50, with bike and helmet hire included and gloves £4.15 to hire (October 2026). The venue says riders must be aged 7 or over, at least 127cm tall, and able to ride a bike without stabilisers.

British Cycling says full-face helmets are compulsory in BMX racing, and full-finger gloves are a must. Cover your arms and legs to protect your skin if you fall, and knee and elbow pads are recommended.
