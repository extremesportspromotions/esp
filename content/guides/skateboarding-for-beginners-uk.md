Skateboarding is standing on a short wooden board with small wheels and moving it by pushing one foot on the ground [1]. A beginner starts by standing on the board on grass or carpet, to get used to standing sideways, then finds a smooth, flat space to roll. Do not go to a skatepark until you can roll with confidence, unless you are there for a lesson [2].

## A first session

Skateboard GB’s learn-to-skate guide sets out those first steps [2]. Skateboard GB describes itself as the national governing body for skateboarding, and Sport England does not list a recognised body for skateboarding in England [4][3].

Press your toes down to turn one way and your heels to turn the other, and stop by pressing your pushing foot on the ground [2]. Left foot forward is called regular, and right foot forward is called goofy, and you use whichever foot feels comfortable at the front [2].

You can also start in a lesson. Find a local skate school, or book private lessons with a Skateboard GB approved instructor [5]. Lessons are mainly at skateparks, or run by skate schools at skateparks or other suitable places, and they cover pushing, turning and learning tricks [6].

## What to take

Buy a proper board from a specialist skate shop, or from a specialist online shop. Toy boards, including many sold by high-street sports shops and toy shops, are poor quality and can put you off [2][7]. A complete board, already built and ready to ride, is a straightforward first board [7]. If you have never stood on a board, a bigger board is usually more stable [7].

Wear skate shoes if you can get them, or trainers with flat soles. Running trainers arch your foot and can throw you off balance [2]. Wear comfortable, loose clothes, and trousers you do not mind ripping, because you may fall while you practise [2].

Wear a helmet while you are learning, and pads can help, especially wrist guards [2]. Pads and guards are recommended because falling is part of learning [8]. Most skate schools and lessons should include a board, a helmet and pads, so ask before you assume you must bring them [9]. Many indoor skateparks rent boards [10].

> [!SAFETY]
> A helmet should fit snug on your head. If it is too loose, it will not protect you [2].
