- **What it is:** British Skydiving's other route to solo skydiving, also called static line. Your parachute opens automatically on your first jumps.
- **What you need first:** You must be at least 16 (under-18s need written permission from a parent or guardian) and start before your 55th birthday. Centres set their own weight limits.
- **What you'll learn:** Exits, canopy control and landings first, then freefall and opening your own parachute.
- **How long it takes:** Usually about 6 hours of ground training before your first jump, then 8 categories at your own pace.
- **Cost:** Paid jump by jump. Ask your centre for its prices.
- **What's next:** [Consolidation and your A licence](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-a-licence-uk)

Picture it: about six hours of ground school, then you're sitting in the door at around 4,000 feet, on your own. You let go, and a static line fixed to the aircraft pulls your parachute open for you. Then it's just you, a steerable square canopy and an instructor on the radio helping you land.

## How it works

The Category System takes you from student to Category 8 in eight levels. Early jumps use the static line. As you progress you lose it, pull your own parachute and make that all-important first freefall. Every level builds the skills you need for your A licence.

It suits you if you'd rather pay as you go than buy a whole course at once.

> [!SAFETY]
> Your instructor clears you for each category. If they ask you to repeat a jump, it's because you're not ready yet, and that's the system keeping you safe.
