- **What it is:** The coach and instructor qualifications that let you teach skydiving.
- **What you need first:** A C Licence to coach FS1 and FF1. Instructor courses need a recommendation from your centre's Chief Instructor.
- **What you'll learn:** How to teach, brief, assess and keep students safe.
- **How long it takes:** Two instructor courses of up to five days each, with at least six months between them (it can be less for tandem).
- **Cost:** Not published by British Skydiving.
- **What's next:** [Competitions](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-competitions-uk)

Everyone remembers their first skydive, and who taught them. This is how you become that person.

## Coaching

British Skydiving's coaching qualification, the Foundation Coach, is accredited by Ofqual and externally quality assured by 1st4Sport. It started in Formation Skydiving, Freefly, Wingsuiting, Tracking and Canopy Formation. In FS, a Foundation Coach coaches people to FS1, and an Advanced FS Coach coaches teams for national and international competition.

## Instructors

There are three ratings, one for each way into the sport. Each starts with a Basic Instructor (BI) course:

- **Category System:** CSBI, then CSI. Needs a C Licence.
- **Tandem:** TBI, then TI. Needs a C Licence, 800 jumps and 8 hours of freefall.
- **AFF:** AFFBI, then AFFI. Needs a D Licence, 1,000 jumps, 10 hours of freefall and a Canopy Handling Coach qualification.

All three need a packing certificate and two years in the sport. If you'll be talking students down by radio, you also need a parachuting radio operator's certificate of competence (ROCC) from the [Civil Aviation Authority](https://www.caa.co.uk/commercial-industry/airspace/air-traffic-management-and-air-navigational-services/licences-for-air-traffic-roles/radio-operators/radio-operators-certificate-of-competence/).

Our advice: the second course is a test, not a lesson. If you fail the closed-book Operations Manual exam, you go home on day one.

> [!SAFETY]
> As an instructor, you may be the one who decides whether someone jumps. Ground them when in doubt.
