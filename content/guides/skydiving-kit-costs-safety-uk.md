Skydiving runs on rules, and they're the reason it stays fun. Every affiliated UK centre trains to British Skydiving's rules.

## Who can jump

- You must be at least 16. Under-18s need written permission from a parent or guardian.
- Solo training must start before your 55th birthday. Some centres set a lower limit. This doesn't apply to tandems, although health rules still do.
- Each centre sets its own weight limits. British Skydiving says they're typically around 15 stone, so ask your centre.

## Membership and insurance

British Skydiving membership includes third-party liability insurance, which covers accidental harm you cause to others, such as damaging property on an off-landing. It doesn't include personal accident cover, and British Skydiving recommends you take out your own. Its cover doesn't apply at indoor wind tunnels.

## Your own kit

Centres provide student kit, and under British Skydiving's rules it must have a reserve static line and an automatic activation device, switched on before every jump. From A Licence up, you must carry a serviceable knife. When you're ready to buy, British Skydiving says to get advice from your Chief Instructor and check the canopy size chart, Form 330i. Cameras come later, at C Licence, with your Chief Instructor's approval.

> [!SAFETY]
> Never jump a canopy smaller than the size chart allows for your experience. In an emergency, call 999: the NHS says 999 is for life-threatening emergencies.
