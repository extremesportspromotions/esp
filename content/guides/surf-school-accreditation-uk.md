- **What it is:** Surfing England's accreditation for surf schools that meet or beat industry best practice.
- **What you need first:** Qualified instructors, insurance, risk assessments, a safeguarding policy and DBS checks.
- **What you'll learn:** What an accredited school runs to, and why it matters on the beach.
- **How long it takes:** An in-person inspection before approval, then a visit every year or two.
- **Cost:** A two-hour group lesson with kit usually costs £45 to £70.
- **What's next:** Learners: [How to start surfing](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/surfing-for-beginners-uk). Instructors: [Surf Instructor: Recreation](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/surf-instructor-recreation-award-uk).

Accreditation is Surfing England's independent stamp for councils, beach owners, insurers and customers. It also says working to industry best practice is a requirement to operate on many beaches.

## What accredited schools show

- Risk assessments and safeguarding policies.
- Insurance details.
- All relevant qualifications.
- DBS checks carried out.

Lessons are risk assessed, run to defined ratios and matched to the surfers' ability.

## What counts as a qualified coach

Surfing England's 2026 guidance says a qualified instructor or surf coach holds current ISA Level 1 or above, or Surfing England's Level 1 Recreational award, plus an in-date beach lifeguard qualification.

Accredited schools also get Surfing England's support on safeguarding, cheaper insurance and access to Sport England funding.

## Wales and Scotland

The Welsh Surfing Federation has a surf school membership, and Scottish Surfing lists accredited surf schools.

> [!SAFETY]
> Caught in a rip? Don't swim against it. Stay with your board, or Float to Live if you don't have one. If you can stand, wade, don't swim. Wade or swim parallel to the shore until you're free, then head for shore. Raise your hand and shout for help. See someone else in trouble? Alert the lifeguards or call 999 or 112 and ask for the coastguard. That's the [RNLI's advice](https://rnli.org/safety/know-the-risks/rip-currents).
