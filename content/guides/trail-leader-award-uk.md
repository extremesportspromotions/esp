- **What it is:** British Cycling's leadership award for trails, developed from the old Level 2 award.
- **What you need first:** British Cycling membership, and you must be 17 to attend (18 to lead on your own). You need to be fit for a day's riding and handle features generally comparable to red-graded trail-centre trails.
- **What you'll learn:** Group management, planning rides, navigation, managing risk and emergencies, trailside repairs and core techniques.
- **How long it takes:** 2 to 3 hours of e-learning and 2 days of training, then an online module and a 1-day assessment. You have up to two years after training to be assessed.
- **Cost:** British Cycling lists training at £370 to £550 and assessment at £250 to £350, set by each provider.
- **What's next:** Leader track: [Mountain Bike Leader Award](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/mountain-bike-leader-award-uk).

Red-grade trails, a group of up to eight and you in charge. British Cycling calls this the entry point for most aspiring off-road leaders.

## Your logbook

On or before assessment day, show evidence of recent rides in suitable terrain. British Cycling recommends at least 15 quality rides, four of them 4 hours or more.

## Once you've passed

To be covered by British Cycling insurance once you've passed, you need to be a current paid British Cycling member, hold a two-day outdoor first aid certificate, and complete safeguarding training appropriate to the country you lead in.

Already hold another leadership award? British Cycling has a Recognition of Prior Learning route to Trail Leader assessment.

> [!SAFETY]
> Ride it before you lead it. Weather and trail conditions change what's sensible for your group.
