> [!SAFETY]
> If someone is hurt, call 999. The NHS says 999 is for life-threatening emergencies, and the call handler will want the location (postcode or landmarks), what has happened and your number. That's from the NHS [When to call 999](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/urgent-and-emergency-care-services/when-to-call-999/) page.

## The rule

The Operations Manual says any modification or repair to a wingsuit that could change how the main or reserve works must be done or inspected by a qualified rigger or the wingsuit manufacturer.

## What the Wingsuit Training Manual recommends

- **Pilot chute:** a BOC throwaway with a kill line. No leg-mounted pilot chutes, because the suit hides them.
- **Canopy:** a larger, docile canopy, preferably square rather than elliptical.
- **Altimeter:** wrist or hand mounted. A chest altimeter can read up to 1,000ft out.
- **AAD:** it may not fire in full flight, because your descent can be slower than its trigger speed.
- **Suit:** stable and easy to fly. The wings must not cover the main handle, and the cutaway and reserve handles must not get sucked into the suit. A little too loose is better than too tight.

The coach can refuse the jump if they're not happy with your kit.

## On the jump

- The manual's first flights start the wave-off at 5,500ft and deploy at 5,000ft.
- Never fly head-on at another wingsuiter. Closing speeds can reach 200mph.
- Stay clear of the aircraft's line of flight and other canopies.
- The manual says it's not permitted to fly more than 1.5 miles from the centre of the drop zone.

## Wingsuit BASE

Wingsuit BASE, jumping from cliffs or buildings, is not part of this pathway. British Skydiving's Operations Manual says fixed-object jumping doesn't come under it, and British Skydiving doesn't associate it with skydiving. Don't treat a wingsuit grade as a step towards it.
