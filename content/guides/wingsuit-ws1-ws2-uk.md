- **What it is:** British Skydiving's two wingsuit grades, WS1 and WS2, and the First Flight Course that leads to WS1.
- **What you need first:** A [C Licence](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-c-licence-uk) and Tracking Grade 1 (TR1), with at least 500 descents, or at least 200 in the previous 18 months.
- **What you'll learn:** How to fly, navigate and deploy safely in a wingsuit, then how to fly with others.
- **Cost:** A first-flight course, coaching plus the jump, is usually £250 to £600, and jump tickets can be extra. A coached jump is usually £40 to £100.
- **What's next:** Competition track: [wingsuit competitions](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/wingsuit-competitions-uk). Safety track: [kit and safety](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/wingsuit-kit-safety-uk). Skydiving track: [D Licence](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-d-licence-uk).

There's no wingsuit flight you can book as a beginner. You start as an experienced skydiver.

## The entry rule

The September 2026 Operations Manual says that to get WS1 you must:

- hold a [C Licence](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-c-licence-uk)
- hold Grade 1 in Tracking (TR1)
- have at least 500 descents, or at least 200 in the previous 18 months

TR1 itself needs a [B Licence](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-b-licence-uk) and FS1. See [disciplines and grades](https://www.extremesportspromotions.com/guides/skydiving-disciplines-and-grades-uk).

## WS1: the First Flight Course

You're introduced by a nominated WS2 skydiver or someone of proven wingsuit instructional ability, and you get a full safety brief. To pass, you show you can:

- fly the suit safely, with overall stability, on at least three descents
- deploy at the correct altitude on at least three descents
- fly a set flight pattern and land within 50 metres of the target
- do the right post-opening checks on every qualifying descent

British Skydiving's Wingsuit Training Manual says the first flight should be one to one with a coach wherever possible. If you have fewer than 500 descents, it asks for at least one jump in a training wingsuit or tracking suit first. The coach can always refuse to train you.

With WS1 you can fly in groups of up to 3, unless a wingsuit coach is on the jump.

## WS2

WS2 adds fall-rate control, moving forwards, backwards and sideways, recovering from an unstable exit, approaching a target, and five consecutive landings within 25 metres. With WS2 and Advanced Instructor approval, you can fly in bigger groups.

> [!SAFETY]
> If someone is hurt, call 999. The NHS says 999 is for life-threatening emergencies, and the call handler will want the location (postcode or landmarks), what has happened and your number. That's from the NHS [When to call 999](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/urgent-and-emergency-care-services/when-to-call-999/) page.

## Not part of this route

Wingsuit BASE, jumping from cliffs or buildings, is not part of this pathway. British Skydiving's Operations Manual says fixed-object jumping doesn't come under it, and British Skydiving doesn't associate it with skydiving.
